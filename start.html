Before you go

A two-minute check that makes every trip better.

  • Water — at least 1 L per 2 hours of walking, more in heat.
  • Layers — a warm and a rain layer, even on a blue-sky day.
  • Food — something salty and something sweet.
  • Phone + power bank — and the route saved for offline use.
  • First-aid basics, sun protection, and a headlamp for long days.
  • Check the trailhead forecast on the Trails page — valleys and summits often differ wildly.
  • Afternoon thunderstorms are a mountain classic in summer: start early, turn around early.
  • Wind on ridges matters more than temperature. 40+ km/h gusts mean hats, poles and care.
  • Share your route and planned return time with a friend (the Share button in the top bar makes it easy).
  • Agree on a “call me by” time — if you’re not back, they know to check in.
  • Going solo often? Our Community page has buddy check-in ideas.

Difficulty, in plain language

Trails use the SAC hiking scale. Here’s what the grades mean when you see them on the Trails page.

GradeWhat it means
T1 · HikingWell-kept path, mostly gentle. Normal trainers are fine.
T2 · MountainSteeper, rooty or rocky in places. Hiking shoes recommended.
T3 · DemandingExposed spots possible, hands occasionally helpful. Sure-footedness needed.
T4+ · AlpineSteep, exposed, sometimes aided by cables or ladders. Experience required.

When a trail isn’t graded, treat it as unknown — start short and see how it feels.
